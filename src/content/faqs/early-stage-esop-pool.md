---
question: "How and when should an early-stage startup create an ESOP pool?"
tags: ["Equity & ESOPs", "Pvt Limited"]
order: 20
---

An **ESOP (Employee Stock Ownership Plan)** pool is a portion of company shares reserved to attract, compensate, and retain key early hires when cash runway is tight.

When to set it up:
- **Don't create it on day 1:** Setting up an ESOP pool requires formal shareholder approval, drafting a policy scheme document, and filing ROC form MGT-14. If you have only co-founders and no immediate senior hires, creating an ESOP pool on day zero creates unnecessary compliance overhead.
- **Set it up when making key senior hires or raising an institutional round:** Early-stage startups usually carve out **7% to 10%** of total equity into an ESOP pool when hiring their first founding engineers, product leads, or when institutional seed investors mandate an unallocated pool.

The legal procedure under Section 62(1)(b) of the Companies Act 2013:
1. **Board Approval:** Board passes a resolution approving the draft ESOP Scheme document.
2. **Shareholder Approval (Special Resolution):** Convene an EGM to approve the scheme by at least a 75% majority.
3. **ROC Filing:** File Form **MGT-14** with the ROC within 30 days of passing the special resolution.
4. **Grant Letters:** Issue individual ESOP grant letters specifying the grant date, exercise price (often face value ₹10 for early staff), and vesting schedule (typically 4 years with 1-year cliff).
