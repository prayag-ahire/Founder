question: "Can a Sole Proprietorship or unregistered partnership get DPIIT Startup Recognition?"

**No. A Sole Proprietorship is strictly ineligible for DPIIT Startup India Recognition.**

Under the official Gazette notification issued by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry (DPIIT), an entity can only qualify as a "Startup" if it is incorporated or registered as one of the following:

1. **Private Limited Company** (under the Companies Act, 2013)
2. **Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)** (under the Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008)
3. **Registered Partnership Firm** (formally registered under Section 59 of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932)

### Why Sole Proprietorships are Disqualified
- A sole proprietorship has **no separate legal personality** from its owner. Assets, liabilities, PAN, and debts belong entirely to the individual.
- Startup India benefits (angel tax exemptions, public tender waivers, and SISFS grants) are designed for corporate entities where equity, ownership, and intellectual property can be transferred or diluted.

### What About Partnership Firms?
- **Unregistered Partnerships:** **Ineligible.** Simply notarizing a partnership deed on ₹500 stamp paper does not qualify.
- **Registered Partnerships:** **Eligible** only if the firm is formally registered with the state **Registrar of Firms (ROF)** and holds a formal Certificate of Registration.

### What Should a Solo Founder Do?
If you currently operate as a sole proprietor or freelancer and want to unlock DPIIT recognition, Startup India seed grants, or patent fee rebates:
- **Incorporate a Private Limited Company or One Person Company (OPC)** via the MCA SPICe+ portal, or
- **Form a Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)** with a family member or trusted partner.

Once your Certificate of Incorporation (CIN / LLPIN) and company PAN arrive, you can submit your DPIIT application immediately on the National Single Window System (NSWS).
