question: "How does an iSAFE note work for Indian startups raising angel funding?"

An **iSAFE** (India Simple Agreement for Future Equity, pioneered by 100X.VC) is the Indian legal adaptation of Y Combinator’s SAFE note. It allows early-stage startups to raise pre-seed and angel capital quickly without fixing an arbitrary valuation before having significant revenue or traction.

Because Indian company law does not have a pure convertible instrument without equity or debt classification, an iSAFE is structured as **Compulsorily Convertible Preference Shares (CCPS)** with a nominal face value (e.g. ₹10 or ₹100 per share) and a tiny preferential dividend (0.0001%).

The key mechanics:
1. **Valuation Cap & Discount:** The investor gives money today. The note converts into ordinary equity shares at the startup’s next qualified institutional round (Series A or priced Seed), usually at a discounted price or capped valuation (whichever is more favorable to the investor).
2. **Speed & Lower Legal Costs:** Standard share-subscription and shareholders' agreements (SHA/SSA) can cost ₹1.5L–₹3L in legal fees and take weeks. An iSAFE agreement is standardized (5–8 pages) and keeps closing fast.
3. **No Debt Pressure:** Unlike convertible debt, an iSAFE does not carry an interest rate or maturity deadline demanding cash repayment if you don't raise in 12 months.

Once closed, you file **Form PAS-3** with the ROC within 30 days of allotting the CCPS.
