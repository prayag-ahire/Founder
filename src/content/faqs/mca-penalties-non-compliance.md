Founders sometimes abandon an incorporated company or ignore ROC filings when a project stalls, assuming "if there is no revenue, there is no harm." In reality, **the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) imposes automatic, compounding daily penalties that attach directly to the company AND the founders personally**.

Here are the real legal and financial consequences of non-compliance:

### 1. Cumulative ₹100 Per Day Late Fees (Per Form)
- Annual filings for Private Limited companies (**AOC-4** and **MGT-7**) and LLPs (**Form 11** and **Form 8**) carry a non-negotiable additional fee of **₹100 per day of delay**.
- Because there is no statutory upper cap on this daily fee, missing filings for two years across four forms can accumulate **over ₹1.5 Lakhs in late fees** before you are allowed to submit the documents or close the entity.

### 2. Disqualification of Directors (Section 164(2) Ban)
If a company fails to file financial statements (AOC-4) or annual returns (MGT-7) for **three consecutive financial years**:
- Every director on the board is **automatically disqualified for a period of 5 years**.
- Their DIN is deactivated.
- They are automatically vacated from the board of **all other companies** where they serve as a director.
- They cannot be appointed as a director in any new company for 5 years.

### 3. Deactivation of DIN (₹5,000 Penalty)
- If you fail to complete the annual **DIR-3 KYC** by 30 September, your DIN is flagged as *Deactivated due to non-filing of DIR-3 KYC*.
- You cannot sign or submit any ROC filings for any company. Re-activating the DIN requires payment of a **mandatory ₹5,000 penalty per director**.

### 4. Non-filing of INC-20A (Commencement of Business)
- If Form INC-20A is not filed within 180 days of incorporation, the company cannot legally conduct any business, issue invoices, or borrow funds.
- The ROC can issue a show-cause notice and initiate **strike-off proceedings** to remove the company from the register.
- Penalty: **₹50,000 on the company** and **₹1,000 per day on every defaulting director** (up to ₹1,00,000).

### 5. Suo-Motu Strike-Off (Section 248) & Frozen Bank Accounts
- If a company fails to carry on business or file returns for 2 consecutive years without applying for *Dormant Status* (Form MSC-1), the Registrar of Companies (ROC) will issue a public notice and strike off the company name from the register.
- The company’s bank accounts are instantly **frozen by banks** upon ROC notification, blocking any remaining company funds.

**Founder Takeaway:** Never leave a registered company unattended. If you decide not to proceed with the venture, either file for formal strike-off (**Form STK-2**) to legally dissolve the entity, or maintain minimal annual filings (AOC-4, MGT-7/7A, DIR-3 KYC) to keep your record and DIN clean.
