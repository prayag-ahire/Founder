When hiring your first team members in India, labor and employment regulations kick in based on employee headcount and state laws. Understanding these thresholds prevents surprise labor department notices.

Here are the primary employee compliances early founders must know:

### 1. Shops and Establishments Act (Gumasta / Trade License)
- **Applicability:** Every commercial establishment or startup office (including co-working spaces in many states like Karnataka, Maharashtra, Delhi, and Telangana).
- **Deadline:** Must be registered with the local municipal or state labor department within **30 days** of starting operations or hiring the first employee.
- **Covers:** Working hours, weekly holidays, overtime compensation, and leave policies.

### 2. Professional Tax (PT)
- A state-level tax on professions, trades, and employments (applicable in Karnataka, Maharashtra, West Bengal, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, etc. — but not in Delhi).
- Requires two registrations:
  - **PTEC (Professional Tax Enrolment Certificate):** Paid by the company/directors for the right to practice business.
  - **PTRC (Professional Tax Registration Certificate):** Enables the company to deduct monthly professional tax (usually ₹200/month) from employees' salaries and remit it to the state government.

### 3. Employee Provident Fund (EPFO) — Threshold: 20 Employees
- **Mandatory:** When your startup reaches **20 or more employees** (including contract and temporary workers).
- **Voluntary:** Startups with fewer than 20 employees can opt in voluntarily.
- **Contribution:** 12% of basic salary + DA contributed by the employee, and a matching 12% contributed by the employer.

### 4. Employee State Insurance (ESIC) — Threshold: 10 Employees
- **Mandatory:** In designated areas, applies to establishments with **10 or more employees** whose gross wages are **₹21,000 per month or less**.
- Provides medical care, sickness benefits, and maternity benefits.
- **Contribution:** 0.75% of wages by the employee and 3.25% by the employer.

### 5. POSH Act (Prevention of Sexual Harassment) — Threshold: 10 Employees
- Under the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace Act 2013, any organisation with **10 or more employees** must mandatorily:
  - Draft and publish an internal POSH policy.
  - Constitute an **Internal Complaints Committee (ICC)** led by a senior woman employee and including an independent external member (from an NGO or legal background).
  - Submit an annual POSH report to the local District Officer.

### 6. Gratuity Act — Applies after 5 Years of Continuous Service
- Payment of Gratuity Act applies to establishments with 10 or more employees. Payable to any employee who completes **5 continuous years** of service upon resignation or retirement (calculated as `15 days' last drawn salary × number of years of service / 26`).

*Tip:* For your first 2–5 hires, many startups engage talent on **Consultancy / Independent Contractor Agreements** with 10% TDS (Section 194J) rather than formal employment contracts, provided the role is project-based and does not violate employment misclassification rules.
