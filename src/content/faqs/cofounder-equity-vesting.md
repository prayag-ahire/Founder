Splitting equity 50/50 upfront with zero vesting is one of the most common ways early-stage startups die. If a co-founder leaves after four months, they walk away with half the company, making the startup completely uninvestable to future angels or VCs.

Best practices for early Indian founders:

1. **Implement 4-Year Vesting with a 1-Year Cliff:**
   Every founder's equity should vest over 48 months. If anyone quits or is terminated within the first 12 months (the "cliff"), they leave with 0% vested shares. After month 12, shares vest monthly or quarterly (e.g. 25% after year one, and 1/48th each subsequent month).

2. **How Vesting Works Under Indian Law (Reverse Vesting):**
   Under Indian Companies Act, shares are allotted upfront to founders at face value (e.g., ₹10/share) to establish promoter control and avoid future gift-tax liabilities. The **Founders' Agreement** and **Articles of Association (AOA)** include a *reverse vesting clawback*: if a founder leaves before 4 years, the company or remaining founders have the legal option to repurchase the unvested shares at face value or transfer them to an employee trust.

3. **Define Good Leaver vs. Bad Leaver:**
   - **Good Leaver:** Leaves due to medical incapacity, death, or mutual board consent — usually keeps already-vested shares.
   - **Bad Leaver:** Terminated for fraud, gross breach of fiduciary duty, or stealing company IP — unvested shares are immediately forfeited and vested shares may be subject to repurchase at fair market value or face value.

Get a signed **Co-founders' Agreement** and IP assignment deed before you write your first line of production code or file SPICe+.
